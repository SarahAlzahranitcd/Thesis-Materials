Abstract

The Mapping Metadata Vocabulary (MMV) is a lifecycle-based ontology for documenting declarative mapping projects used to generate knowledge graphs and Linked Data. It captures contextual, design, implementation, testing, and maintenance metadata to support mapping understanding, assessment, and reuse. MMV complements existing provenance and quality vocabularies by focusing on mapping-specific lifecycle information.

Introduction back to ToC

Declarative mappings (e.g., RML mappings, ontology alignments, and interlinking rules) are central to generating RDF and knowledge graphs from heterogeneous data sources. However, mapping artefacts are typically shared as standalone technical files with limited contextual documentation. While executable, such artefacts often lack structured metadata describing their purpose, design decisions, validation results, and maintenance history. This limits transparency, evaluation, governance, and informed reuse. Existing vocabularies such as PROV-O and DQV support provenance and quality description, but they do not explicitly capture the lifecycle context of mapping projects nor provide mapping-specific metadata constructs that integrate analysis, design, development, testing, and maintenance information. The Mapping Metadata Vocabulary (MMV) addresses this gap by introducing a lifecycle-based model for documenting declarative mapping projects. MMV structures metadata across five lifecycle phases:
  • - Analysis: purpose, requirements, assumptions, and input datasets
  • - Design: modelling decisions and justifications
  • - Development: implementation details and methods
  • - Testing: validation activities and quality assessment
  • - Maintenance: versioning and publication information
By organising metadata around lifecycle activities, MMV moves beyond artefact-centric documentation toward a process-aware representation that improves mapping understanding, assessment, and reuse. MMV complements existing standards rather than replacing them. It reuses established Semantic Web vocabularies (e.g., PROV-O, DQV, FOAF, MQV) while specialising in mapping-oriented lifecycle metadata. The vocabulary is intended for knowledge engineers, ontology engineers, and researchers who require structured, transparent documentation of mapping artefacts. Version 0.2 refines the vocabulary based on expert feedback. It introduces an explicit ValidationReport class linked to TestingActivity through hasValidationReport, enriches stakeholder modelling with foaf:homepage and foaf:organization, and adds prov:wasRevisionOf for tracking version provenance across mapping artefacts.

Scope

The Mapping Metadata Vocabulary defines a focused set of constructs for documenting declarative mapping projects across the lifecycle. Its scope is summarised below.

In scope.

  • Lifecycle metadata for declarative mapping projects, organised across analysis, design, development, testing, and maintenance phases.
  • Mapping artefact metadata including type, format, method, version, persistent identifier, and revision history.
  • Validation evidence linking testing activities to validation reports (e.g., SHACL reports).
  • Stakeholder roles, backgrounds, and organisational affiliations.
  • Alignment with established Semantic Web vocabularies including PROV-O, DQV, FOAF, and MQV.

Out of scope.

  • The executable mapping syntax itself. MMV documents mapping artefacts but does not redefine languages such as RML, R2RML, or SSSOM.
  • Mapping execution semantics or runtime behaviour.
  • Full publication of input or output datasets. MMV records descriptive metadata about inputs to support understanding and reuse.
  • General data quality assessment beyond the mapping context. MMV reuses DQV terms where relevant rather than reinventing quality measurement.

Competency Questions back to ToC

Competency questions (CQs) are used as a design validation instrument for MMV. They specify the information needs the vocabulary is intended to support, particularly around interpretation, assessment, reuse, and maintenance of declarative mapping projects. The CQs were authored by synthesising gaps identified in the literature on mapping documentation and recurring questions that practitioners pose when encountering an existing mapping artefact. During design, MMV concepts and relationships were introduced, refined, or excluded based on their contribution to answering one or more competency questions.

Table 2 presents the representative set of competency questions used to guide the design of MMV. Each CQ is paired with the MMV classes and properties that enable it to be answered.

Table 2: Representative competency questions and the MMV terms that support them
CQCompetency QuestionSupporting MMV Classes / Properties
CQ1 What is the purpose and scope of the mapping project? mmv:MappingProject, mmv:AnalysisActivity, mmv:hasPurpose, mmv:hasRequirement, mmv:hasMappingDomain
CQ2 Which mapping type is being documented? mmv:MappingArtifact, mqv:hasMappingType
CQ3 What assumptions or requirements does the mapping rely on? mmv:AnalysisActivity, mmv:hasAssumption, mmv:hasDomainAssumption, mmv:hasRequirement, mmv:hasTechnicalRequirement, mmv:hasRiskOrIssue
CQ4 Which lifecycle activities are associated with the mapping project? mmv:MappingProject, mmv:hasAnalysis, mmv:hasDesign, mmv:hasDevelopment, mmv:hasTesting, mmv:hasMaintenance
CQ5 What key design decisions were made and why? mmv:DesignActivity, mmv:hasDesignDecision, mmv:hasJustification, mmv:hasPlannedQualityMetric
CQ6 Which mapping artefacts were produced and how are they described? mmv:MappingArtifact, mmv:hasMappingArtifact, mmv:version, mmv:hasDescription, mqv:hasMappingType, mqv:hasMappingFormat
CQ7 Which mapping language and format were used? mmv:DevelopmentActivity, mqv:hasMappingFormat, mqv:hasMappingMethod
CQ8 Which tools or methods were used during development? mmv:DevelopmentActivity, mmv:hasTool, mmv:hasMappingAlgorithm
CQ9 How was the mapping tested and what were the results? mmv:TestingActivity, mmv:hasTestingType, mmv:hasTestingResult, mmv:QualityAssessment, mmv:hasQualityMeasurement, mmv:hasValidationReport, mmv:ValidationReport
CQ10 How is the mapping maintained and versioned over time? mmv:MaintenanceActivity, mmv:hasPublisherName, mmv:hasPublisherSource, mmv:version, mmv:hasVersionDateTime, prov:wasRevisionOf

Example SPARQL query for CQ1 (purpose and scope):


PREFIX mmv: <http://w3id.org/mmv/0.2#>

SELECT ?project ?purpose ?domain WHERE {
  ?project a mmv:MappingProject ;
           mmv:hasAnalysis ?analysis .
  ?analysis mmv:hasPurpose ?purpose .
  OPTIONAL { ?analysis mmv:hasMappingDomain ?domain }
}

Example SPARQL query for CQ9 (testing results, including the v0.2 ValidationReport):


PREFIX mmv: <http://w3id.org/mmv/0.2#>
PREFIX dcterms: <http://purl.org/dc/terms/>

SELECT ?project ?testType ?result ?reportLocation WHERE {
  ?project mmv:hasTesting ?testing .
  OPTIONAL { ?testing mmv:hasTestingType ?testType }
  OPTIONAL { ?testing mmv:hasTestingResult ?result }
  OPTIONAL { ?testing mmv:hasValidationReport ?report .
             ?report dcterms:identifier ?reportLocation }
}

The complete set of competency questions, together with their detailed mappings to MMV classes and properties and example SPARQL query templates used during validation, is provided in Appendix B of the thesis (Alzahrani, 2026).

FAIR Alignment back to ToC

MMV is designed to support the documentation of declarative mappings as FAIR research artefacts. Table 3 summarises how MMV constructs support each FAIR principle (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable).

Table 3: How MMV supports the FAIR principles
FAIR PrincipleHow MMV Supports ItRelevant Constructs
Findable Mapping artefacts can be uniquely and persistently identified, described, and discovered through structured lifecycle metadata. mmv:hasIdentifier, mmv:hasDescription, mmv:hasMappingDomain, mmv:version, mmv:hasVersionDateTime
Accessible Mapping artefact metadata records publication and provenance information so that artefacts can be located and retrieved over time. The MMV vocabulary itself is published under an open licence with multiple serialisations. mmv:hasPublisherName, mmv:hasPublisherSource, mmv:MaintenanceActivity
Interoperable MMV reuses established Semantic Web vocabularies rather than reinventing terms, supporting integration with existing provenance, quality, and agent-description ecosystems. Reuse of prov:Activity, prov:Entity, prov:Agent, prov:wasRevisionOf, dqv:Metric, dqv:QualityMeasurement, foaf:Person, foaf:homepage, mqv:hasMappingType, mqv:hasMappingFormat, mqv:hasMappingMethod
Reusable MMV captures the contextual and procedural information required for informed reuse, including purpose, design decisions, assumptions, validation outcomes, and version history. mmv:hasPurpose, mmv:hasDesignDecision, mmv:hasJustification, mmv:hasAssumption, mmv:hasDomainAssumption, mmv:hasTestingResult, mmv:hasValidationReport, mmv:ValidationReport

Usage Example back to ToC

Example: Lifecycle-Based Uplift Mapping from CSV to RDF (v0.2)

This example illustrates how MMV v0.2 can be used to document a lifecycle-based mapping project that transforms a CSV dataset into RDF (e.g., using RML). It demonstrates how a mmv:MappingProject links to its lifecycle phases (Analysis, Design, Development, Testing, and Maintenance), records key metadata, and captures stakeholder information. The example also shows the new v0.2 features: an explicit mmv:ValidationReport linked from a testing activity, enriched stakeholder modelling using foaf:homepage and foaf:organization, and version provenance via prov:wasRevisionOf.

Example in Turtle


@prefix mmv: <http://w3id.org/mmv/0.2#> .
@prefix mqv: <https://alex-randles.github.io/MQV/#> .
@prefix prov: <http://www.w3.org/ns/prov#> .
@prefix dqv: <http://www.w3.org/ns/dqv#> .
@prefix dcterms: <http://purl.org/dc/terms/> .
@prefix foaf: <http://xmlns.com/foaf/0.1/> .
@prefix ex: <http://example.org/> .
@prefix xsd: <http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#> .

# Mapping Project
ex:Project1 a mmv:MappingProject ;
    dcterms:title "Electoral Division Mapping Project" ;
    mmv:hasMappingArtifact ex:MappingV1 ;
    mmv:hasAnalysis ex:Analysis1 ;
    mmv:hasDesign ex:Design1 ;
    mmv:hasDevelopment ex:Development1 ;
    mmv:hasTesting ex:Testing1 ;
    mmv:hasMaintenance ex:Maintenance1 ;
    mmv:hasStakeholder ex:Sarah .

# Mapping Artifact (current version, revision of an earlier draft)
ex:MappingV1 a mmv:MappingArtifact ;
    dcterms:title "Electoral Division Uplift Mapping v1" ;
    mqv:hasMappingType "Uplift Mapping" ;
    mqv:hasMappingMethod "Semi-automatic with manual validation" ;
    mqv:hasMappingFormat "RML" ;
    mmv:version "1.0" ;
    mmv:hasDescription "Transforms CSV electoral division data into RDF using RML." ;
    prov:wasRevisionOf ex:MappingV0 .

ex:MappingV0 a mmv:MappingArtifact ;
    dcterms:title "Electoral Division Uplift Mapping (draft)" ;
    mmv:version "0.9" .

# Analysis Phase
ex:Analysis1 a mmv:AnalysisActivity ;
    mmv:hasPurpose "Transform CSV dataset into RDF for Linked Data publication." ;
    mmv:hasRequirement "All divisions must receive persistent URIs." ;
    mmv:hasMappingDomain "Geospatial statistics" ;
    mmv:hasDomainAssumption "Each division has a unique code." ;
    mmv:hasTechnicalRequirement "Mapping tool must support RDF-star." ;
    mmv:hasInputName "electoral_divisions_2022.csv" ;
    mmv:hasInputDescription "CSV dataset containing electoral division boundaries and codes." .

# Design Phase
ex:Design1 a mmv:DesignActivity ;
    mmv:hasDesignDecision "Reuse GeoSPARQL vocabulary for geometry modelling." ;
    mmv:hasJustification "Ensures interoperability with geospatial Linked Data systems." ;
    mmv:hasPlannedQualityMetric ex:CompletenessMetric .

ex:CompletenessMetric a dqv:Metric .

# Development Phase
ex:Development1 a mmv:DevelopmentActivity ;
    prov:startedAtTime "2025-06-01"^^xsd:date ;
    prov:endedAtTime "2025-06-06"^^xsd:date .

# Testing Phase (with explicit ValidationReport, new in v0.2)
ex:Testing1 a mmv:TestingActivity ;
    mmv:hasTestingType "SHACL validation" ;
    mmv:hasTestingResult "All constraints passed." ;
    mmv:hasValidationReport ex:ShaclReport1 ;
    prov:generatedAtTime "2025-06-07T15:00:00"^^xsd:dateTime .

ex:ShaclReport1 a mmv:ValidationReport ;
    dcterms:identifier <https://example.org/reports/shacl-2025-06-07.ttl> ;
    dcterms:description "SHACL validation report covering structural and value constraints." .

# Maintenance Phase
ex:Maintenance1 a mmv:MaintenanceActivity ;
    mmv:hasPublisherName "Trinity College Dublin" ;
    dcterms:issued "2025-06-07T18:30:00"^^xsd:dateTime .

# Stakeholder (enriched with FOAF properties, new in v0.2)
ex:Sarah a foaf:Person ;
    foaf:name "Sarah Alzahrani" ;
    foaf:homepage <https://sarahalzahranitcd.github.io/> ;
    foaf:organization "Trinity College Dublin" ;
    mmv:hasRole "Mapping Developer" ;
    mmv:hasBackground "Researcher in knowledge graph engineering and semantic mappings." .

In this example, the mapping project links to lifecycle phases using object properties such as mmv:hasAnalysis, mmv:hasDesign, and mmv:hasTesting. Each lifecycle phase records relevant metadata using MMV data properties (e.g., purpose, requirements, design decisions, testing outcomes, and versioning information), supporting transparency, evaluation, and informed reuse of mapping artefacts. The v0.2 additions show how a SHACL validation report is preserved as a first-class entity, how stakeholder profiles can carry richer FOAF context, and how version revisions across mapping artefacts are recorded using prov:wasRevisionOf.

Evaluation back to ToC

MMV has been evaluated through expert review (Study 3 of the thesis), in which domain experts assessed the vocabulary against its competency questions, requirements, and intended usage scenarios. The expert evaluation focused on coverage of mapping lifecycle metadata, fitness for documenting realistic mapping projects, and alignment with established Semantic Web vocabularies. Feedback from this evaluation informed the v0.2 refinements documented in the changelog, including the introduction of mmv:ValidationReport, the enrichment of stakeholder modelling, and the addition of revision provenance.

Earlier studies in the thesis provide complementary empirical foundations for MMV. Study 1 (community survey) identified the metadata requirements that MMV operationalises [2]. Study 2 (usability evaluation of the MetaSEMAP tool) assessed the practicability of structured lifecycle metadata annotation [4]. Study 5 examined the use of lifecycle metadata to guide LLM-based mapping generation [5]. The design and instantiation of MMV are reported in the ICSC 2026 paper [6].

Full details of the studies and their results are provided in the thesis (Alzahrani, 2026).
